<h2 id="what">What is ABDM, actually?</h2>
<p>
	<strong>ABDM</strong> stands for the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission — the National Health
	Authority's framework for letting Indian health records flow between providers, with the patient's
	consent, on standard rails. It is not a database. It is a set of agreements about how databases
	talk to each other.
</p>
<p>Three things make it real:</p>
<ul>
	<li>
		An ID for the patient — <strong>ABHA</strong> (Ayushman Bharat Health Account), a 14-digit
		number.
	</li>
	<li>
		An ID for the provider — the Healthcare Professional Registry (HPR) and Health Facility Registry
		(HFR).
	</li>
	<li>A consent layer — the patient grants and revokes access per provider, per visit.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="abha">What is ABHA, in plain language?</h2>
<p>
	ABHA is the patient's lifelong health ID. It is created by the patient — not the hospital — in
	under 90 seconds via the ABHA app or the ABDM portal, with mobile + Aadhaar (optional). It comes
	in two forms the front desk should tell apart — a <a href="/in/blog/abha-number-vs-address/">14-digit
	ABHA number and an ABHA address</a>.
</p>
<p>
	Behind the ID is consent-based access to past records. The patient grants permission per provider,
	per visit. Hospital A can read what Hospital B wrote, but only if the patient said yes.
</p>
<blockquote>
	The records stay where they were created. ABHA is the index. Nothing moves until the patient moves
	it.
</blockquote>

<h2 id="hpr-hfr">What are HPR and HFR?</h2>
<p>
	HPR is the registry of healthcare professionals — doctors with verified medical council
	registration numbers. HFR is the registry of health facilities — hospitals, clinics, diagnostic
	centres with verified addresses. Both are public, both are queryable.
</p>
<p>
	The moment a provider is HPR-registered and the facility is HFR-registered, your clinic can issue
	ABHA-linked records and verify other providers. Setup takes about an hour per doctor.
</p>

<h2 id="why-now">Why does this matter in 2026 (and not in 2024)?</h2>
<p>Three things changed:</p>
<ol>
	<li>
		Several state insurance schemes have moved to ABHA-linked claims processing. Without it, you are
		filing on paper.
	</li>
	<li>
		PMJAY claims at NABH-empanelled hospitals are increasingly ABHA-linked. The threshold of
		“optional” is being pulled back.
	</li>
	<li>
		The Unified Health Interface (UHI) lets cross-clinic referrals work — a derm clinic in Lucknow
		can refer to a tertiary centre in Kolkata, with consent, without a fax.
	</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="what-you-do">What should a clinic do now?</h2>
<p>Two things, in order:</p>
<ol>
	<li>
		Get the facility <a href="/in/blog/register-clinic-on-hfr/">registered on the HFR</a>. The
		doctors HPR-registered. ~60 minutes total.
	</li>
	<li>
		At first visit, offer to create or link ABHA for the patient. Most patients say yes; the few who
		don't, you skip without friction.
	</li>
</ol>
<p>
	If your software vendor can't do both of these natively, ask when. If they can't answer — that's
	your answer.
</p>

<h2 id="what-its-not">What ABDM is not</h2>
<p>
	It's not a national medical record system. It's not a government database of your patient's data.
	It's not a mandate that you upload anything — records stay with the provider that created them.
	It's not a substitute for your EMR — your EMR has to <em>support</em> ABDM. The two work together.
</p>
