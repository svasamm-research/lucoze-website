<h2 id="what-onboarding-involves">What does ABDM onboarding actually involve?</h2>
<p>
	Most owners hear "get ABDM-ready" and picture one form. It's three separate jobs, run by the{" "}
	<a href="https://nha.gov.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">
		National Health Authority (NHA)
	</a>
	, and they live on three different portals:
</p>
<ul>
	<li>
		<strong>HFR</strong> — the Health Facility Registry. Registers the <em>place</em>: your clinic,
		nursing home, or diagnostic centre.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>HPR</strong> — the Healthcare Professionals Registry. Registers the <em>people</em>:
		each doctor, with their verified council number.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>ABHA</strong> — the patient's health ID, created or linked at your front desk once the
		first two are done.
	</li>
</ul>
<p>
	Do them in that order and the path is clean. Do them out of order — chase ABHA linking before the
	facility is registered, say — and you hit walls. If you want the background on what ABDM is and
	why any of this matters, start with our{" "}
	<a href="/in/blog/abdm-abha-guide/">ABDM &amp; ABHA guide</a>; this post is the connective piece
	that walks the whole onboarding, end to end.
</p>

<h2 id="register-the-facility">How do you register the facility on HFR?</h2>
<p>
	HFR is the row that makes your clinic real on the network — a verified facility ID that lets you
	issue and verify ABHA-linked records. We've written the full walkthrough separately, so here's the
	short version: you go to{" "}
	<a href="https://facility.abdm.gov.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">
		facility.abdm.gov.in
	</a>
	, sign in with a mobile OTP, enter your facility's type, ownership, address, and the identifiers
	the form asks for, and submit for verification. HFR entries are verified, not self-asserted, so
	there's a review step before the facility is fully live.
</p>
<p>
	The two things people get wrong are picking the wrong facility type (the form branches on it) and
	entering an address that doesn't match reality (verification checks it). For the exact documents,
	fields, and current steps, read the dedicated guide:{" "}
	<a href="/in/blog/register-clinic-on-hfr/">how to register your clinic on HFR</a>. Get the
	facility ID first — the doctor registrations and everything downstream reference it.
</p>

<h2 id="register-doctors-on-hpr">How do doctors register on HPR?</h2>
<p>
	This is the piece most owners haven't done, so it's worth walking through. HPR is the registry of
	verified healthcare professionals — each doctor gets a Healthcare Professional ID tied to their
	medical (or other council) registration. It's the professional-side twin of HFR: the facility is
	the place, the HPR ID is the person. It's registered per doctor, not per clinic, and it's free.
</p>
<p>
	Registration happens on{" "}
	<a href="https://hpr.abdm.gov.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">
		hpr.abdm.gov.in
	</a>
	. The portal is the source of truth and the screens do change, so treat this as the shape of the
	flow rather than a script. Broadly, each doctor:
</p>
<ul>
	<li>
		Goes to the HPR portal and starts registration as a healthcare professional, creating their
		Healthcare Professional ID.
	</li>
	<li>
		Verifies identity — commonly via Aadhaar OTP on an Aadhaar-linked mobile — and sets a username
		and password.
	</li>
	<li>
		Selects their professional category and system of medicine (for example, modern medicine), then
		enters qualifications and their <strong>council registration details exactly as printed</strong>{" "}
		on the certificate.
	</li>
	<li>
		Uploads the supporting proof the form asks for — typically a photograph, degree and council
		certificates, and a signature — and declares where they currently practise.
	</li>
	<li>
		Submits the profile for verification against council records. The HPR ID becomes active once
		it's approved.
	</li>
</ul>
<p>
	A few things worth knowing before your doctors sit down to do it. The registration detail has to
	match the council record precisely — a mismatched number or a name spelt differently on the
	certificate is the usual reason a profile stalls. Each doctor does their own, with their own
	Aadhaar and their own council number; the clinic owner can't register a doctor's HPR ID{" "}
	<em>as</em> them. And the exact document list and any timelines are set by the portal and vary —
	don't take a forum post (or this one) as the final word. Check{" "}
	<a href="https://hpr.abdm.gov.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">
		hpr.abdm.gov.in
	</a>{" "}
	for the current requirements.
</p>

<h2 id="going-live">How do you actually go live with ABHA?</h2>
<p>
	Once the facility is on HFR and your doctors are on HPR, you're on the map. Going live means one
	thing operationally: at the front desk, you start creating or linking a patient's ABHA and
	connecting their visit records to it — with their consent, one visit at a time.
</p>
<p>
	An <strong>ABHA</strong> (Ayushman Bharat Health Account) is the patient's 14-digit health ID. It
	belongs to the patient, is usually created with just a mobile number and an OTP, and doesn't hold
	records itself — it's the index that lets records move between providers when the patient allows
	it. The cleanest moment to offer it is at registration, when you already have the patient's
	attention and their phone in hand. We've written the front-desk version of this in detail:{" "}
	<a href="/in/blog/abha-first-visit-front-desk/">creating an ABHA at the first visit</a>.
</p>
<p>
	The order matters here. You can only link records to a patient's ABHA from a registered facility,
	and ABDM-linked prescriptions lean on the doctor's HPR ID. That's why ABHA is step three, not step
	one: the facility and the professionals have to exist on the registries first, then the front-desk
	flow has something real to connect to.
</p>

<h2 id="incentives">Is there any incentive for going through all this?</h2>
<p>
	Yes — the NHA runs a <strong>Digital Health Incentive Scheme (DHIS)</strong> under ABDM, which
	pays eligible health facilities and digital-solution providers for creating and linking digital
	health records to ABHA. The specifics — who qualifies, the per-record amounts, caps, and how you
	claim — are set by the NHA and have been revised over time, so we won't quote figures that might
	be stale. If the economics matter to your decision, confirm the current terms directly with the{" "}
	<a href="https://nha.gov.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">
		NHA
	</a>{" "}
	and the{" "}
	<a href="https://abdm.gov.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">
		ABDM
	</a>{" "}
	portal before you count on anything.
</p>
<p>
	Our honest take: treat any incentive as a bonus, not the reason to onboard. The real reason is
	that state insurance and scheme workflows increasingly key off ABDM identifiers, and being
	registered keeps you in the digital lane instead of the paper one. The incentive is nice; being on
	the network is the point. For what "ABDM-ready" should actually mean when a vendor says it, see{" "}
	<a href="/in/blog/abdm-ready-meaning/">what ABDM-ready really means</a>.
</p>

<h2 id="checklist">What's the whole path in one list?</h2>
<ul>
	<li>
		<strong>1. Facility on HFR</strong> — register at facility.abdm.gov.in, get the verified
		facility ID. (<a href="/in/blog/register-clinic-on-hfr/">walkthrough</a>)
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>2. Each doctor on HPR</strong> — every practitioner registers at hpr.abdm.gov.in with
		their own council number and Aadhaar, gets a verified HPR ID.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>3. ABHA at the front desk</strong> — start creating/linking ABHA at first visit, with
		consent. (<a href="/in/blog/abha-first-visit-front-desk/">walkthrough</a>)
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>4. Wire the IDs into your software</strong> — so the facility and professional IDs flow
		into records and prescriptions, and linking actually happens.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>5. Check the incentive terms</strong> — if DHIS applies to you, confirm current terms
		with the NHA.
	</li>
</ul>
<p>
	None of the five is hard on its own. The mistake is doing them piecemeal, or stopping at step one
	and calling it done. Being in a registry only pays off when your systems use the IDs every day.
</p>
