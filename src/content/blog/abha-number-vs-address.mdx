<h2 id="what">What is an ABHA?</h2>
<p>
	An <strong>ABHA</strong> — Ayushman Bharat Health Account — is the patient's identity under the{" "}
	<a href="/in/blog/abdm-abha-guide/">Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM)</a>. It's how a person
	collects their health records from different hospitals into one place they control. But an ABHA
	comes in two parts that get confused constantly: the ABHA <strong>number</strong> and the ABHA{" "}
	<strong>address</strong>.
</p>

<h2 id="number">The ABHA number (the 14-digit ID)</h2>
<p>
	The <strong>ABHA number</strong> is a <strong>14-digit unique identifier</strong> — think of it as
	the patient's permanent health ID, the way a UPI-linked bank account has an account number. It
	uniquely identifies the individual across ABDM. It's stable and it's theirs for life.
</p>

<h2 id="address">The ABHA address (the handle)</h2>
<p>
	The <strong>ABHA address</strong> is a self-chosen <strong>username</strong> that looks like an
	email — for example <code>ananya@abdm</code>. It's the handle a patient shares to <em>receive</em>
	records into their account and to consent to sharing them. If the 14-digit number is the account,
	the address is the "@handle" you actually hand over — much like a UPI ID sits on top of a bank
	account number.
</p>
<p>A person can have more than one ABHA address, but they map back to the one ABHA number.</p>

<h2 id="which">Which one does the front desk use?</h2>
<p>
	Mostly the <strong>ABHA address</strong> (or a linked mobile/scan), because that's what a record
	is sent to and what consent is tied to. The number sits underneath as the permanent identity. What
	matters operationally is simply: capture or create the ABHA at registration, and issue the visit's
	records to it — we walk through that in{" "}
	<a href="/in/blog/abha-first-visit-front-desk/">creating an ABHA at the first visit</a>.
</p>
<p>
	The reason a hospital cares at all: your{" "}
	<a href="/in/blog/hfr-explained/">HFR-registered facility</a> and{" "}
	<a href="/in/blog/register-doctors-on-hpr/">HPR-registered doctors</a> issue records; the
	patient's ABHA is where those records land. All three have to line up for ABDM to actually work.
</p>
