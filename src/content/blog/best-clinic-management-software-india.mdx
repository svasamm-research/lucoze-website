<p>
	Search for the best clinic management software in India and you will find lists of twenty
	products, each one "the best". None of them has seen your clinic. A two-doctor family practice and
	a twelve-doctor polyclinic with a pharmacy and a collection centre do not need the same system,
	and the one at the top of a list may be the one that slows your front desk on a Monday morning.
</p>
<p>
	This guide is for the doctor who owns a clinic, or the person who runs one, with one to fifteen
	doctors. It says what to compare, what to ask in a demo, and how to switch without losing a day of
	patients.
</p>

<h2 id="fit">Start with your clinic, not the product list</h2>
<p>Write down four things before you look at any product. The shortlist follows from them.</p>
<ul>
	<li>
		<strong>Doctors and branches.</strong> How many doctors, which specialties, whether any doctor
		sits in two branches on different days.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>How patients come.</strong> Booked by phone, booked on WhatsApp, or walking in. Most
		clinics see all three in the same hour.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>What else you sell.</strong> A pharmacy counter, a collection centre, procedures. Each
		one is a bill that must come from the same patient record.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>What you run today.</strong> A paper register, a spreadsheet, an older clinic software,
		or a mix. This decides how the switch has to be planned.
	</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="compare">What to compare, part by part</h2>
<p>These are the parts of a clinic's day where software makes the biggest difference.</p>
<ul>
	<li>
		<strong>Registration</strong>: one record per patient from the first visit, even when a family
		shares one mobile number, with allergies recorded once and shown to every doctor.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>Appointments and schedules</strong>: each doctor's sessions, leave and slots, across
		branches, so nobody is booked into a day the doctor is away.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>The queue</strong>: phone, WhatsApp and walk-in patients in one token list, with vitals
		taken before the doctor calls them in.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>The consultation</strong>: complaints and diagnoses, orders, and a prescription the
		system checks against the patient's recorded allergies.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>Billing</strong>: consultation and registration fees, procedures, discounts with a
		reason and an approver, refunds that leave a credit note rather than an edited bill, and GST on
		the services that carry it.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>The owner's view</strong>: today's patients, collections and dues, on one screen, at the
		end of the day.
	</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="pharmacy">The pharmacy counter</h2>
<p>
	Many clinics buy separate pharmacy billing software for the counter downstairs. It works, but the
	prescription is typed twice, stock and sales sit apart from the patient's record, and the owner
	adds two sets of collections every evening.
</p>
<p>
	If you run a counter, ask each vendor to take a prescription from a consultation straight to a
	pharmacy bill. That bill must carry the HSN code, batch, expiry and the right GST rate on every
	line, and Schedule H medicines need their own record.{" "}
	<a href="/in/blog/pharmacy-billing-gst/">Pharmacy billing and GST</a> sets out what goes on the
	invoice.
</p>

<h2 id="questions">The questions to ask in every demo</h2>
<p>
	A demo shows the vendor's best path. These questions make it show yours. Bring last week's
	register and ask the vendor to run three of your patients through the system in front of you.
</p>
<ol>
	<li>
		<strong>Register two members of one family on the same mobile number.</strong> Do they stay two
		patients, or does one overwrite the other?
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>Book a patient for a doctor who is on leave that day.</strong> Does the system stop it?
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>Take a walk-in between two booked patients</strong>, record vitals, and send them in.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>Prescribe a medicine the patient is allergic to.</strong> What does the system do?
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>Give a discount, then refund one line of a paid bill.</strong> Who approved the
		discount, and what does the refund leave behind?
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>Show me today's collections</strong>, consultation and pharmacy together.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>How does my data come out if I leave?</strong> In what format, how fast, and at what
		cost.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>How do we move from what we use today?</strong> Who moves the patient list, what does it
		cost, and can the old system stay on while we check?
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>What does it cost for my doctors and branches, per year</strong>, with setup and
		training?
	</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="switch">How to switch without losing a day</h2>
<p>
	Most clinics stay on software they dislike because they fear the switch. A careful switch takes
	the fear away.
</p>
<ul>
	<li>
		<strong>Move the patient list first</strong>, and check it against the old system before anyone
		uses the new one.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>Start at the front desk</strong>, keep the old system on for a week, and compare the
		day's bills until they agree.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>Train on your own patients</strong>, not a demo clinic.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>Retire the old system last</strong>, when nobody has opened it for a week.
	</li>
</ul>
