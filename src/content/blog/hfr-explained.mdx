<h2 id="full-form">What does HFR stand for?</h2>
<p>
	<strong>HFR</strong> stands for <strong>Health Facility Registry</strong>. It is the official,
	verified list of health facilities under the{" "}
	<a href="/in/blog/abdm-abha-guide/">Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM)</a>, run by the
	National Health Authority (NHA). Every hospital, nursing home, clinic, diagnostic centre, lab or
	pharmacy that delivers care can hold one entry, each with its own unique facility ID.
</p>
<p>
	The simplest way to hold it: if ABHA is the patient's health ID, the HFR is the <em>place's</em>{" "}
	ID. One identifies the person; the other identifies where the care happened.
</p>

<h2 id="hfr-id">What is an HFR ID?</h2>
<p>
	When a facility is registered and verified, the HFR issues it a unique <strong>HFR ID</strong> — a
	permanent identifier that stands for that facility across ABDM. It does not expire when your staff
	change or your software changes; it belongs to the facility.
</p>
<p>That ID is what actually does the work. With a verified HFR entry, a hospital can:</p>
<ul>
	<li>
		Issue records — a discharge summary, a prescription, a lab report — that a patient can pull into
		their ABHA.
	</li>
	<li>Be discovered inside ABDM as a real, verified facility rather than a name someone typed.</li>
	<li>
		Verify other providers and take part in consent-based record exchange on the standard rails.
	</li>
</ul>
<p>
	A concrete version: a 60-bed hospital's HFR ID is the thing that lets its discharge summary land
	in the patient's ABHA instead of a paper folder the patient loses by the next visit.
</p>

<h2 id="portal">The HFR portal and login</h2>
<p>
	Registration and management happen on the HFR portal at{" "}
	<a href="https://facility.abdm.gov.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">
		facility.abdm.gov.in
	</a>
	. You sign in against ABDM (typically with a mobile number and OTP), then create or manage your
	facility's entry from there. Portals get redesigned, so treat the exact screens as the shape of
	the thing and confirm the current flow on the portal itself.
</p>
<p>
	One point of confusion worth clearing: the HFR login is the <em>facility</em> account. The doctors
	are registered separately, on the HPR (more on that below), and patients create their ABHA on
	their own — three different registries, three different logins.
</p>

<h2 id="who-needs">Does a hospital actually need to be on the HFR?</h2>
<p>
	If you want any of the things above — issuing ABHA-linked records, being discoverable, joining
	consent-based exchange, or claiming under schemes and incentives that increasingly assume ABDM
	participation — then yes. It is fast becoming the price of entry, not a nice-to-have.
</p>
<p>
	And here is the line that trips up procurement:{" "}
	<strong>"ABDM-ready" software is not the same as being on the HFR.</strong> A vendor can
	truthfully say their product supports ABDM while your facility still has no HFR entry and issues
	nothing. The badge is the software's; the registry entry is yours. Ask for both.
</p>

<h2 id="vs-hpr-abha">HFR vs HPR vs ABHA — the three you keep hearing together</h2>
<p>They are separate registries, and mixing them up is the most common ABDM mistake:</p>
<ul>
	<li>
		<strong>HFR — Health Facility Registry:</strong> registers the <strong>facility</strong> (your
		hospital) and gives it the HFR ID.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>HPR — Healthcare Professionals Registry:</strong> registers the{" "}
		<strong>doctors and clinicians</strong>, separately from the facility.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>ABHA — Ayushman Bharat Health Account:</strong> the <strong>patient's</strong> health
		ID, created or linked at the front desk.
	</li>
</ul>
<p>
	A hospital that is truly on ABDM has all three moving: the facility on HFR, its clinicians on HPR,
	and ABHAs being created at registration. We go through the whole sequence in{" "}
	<a href="/in/blog/abdm-onboarding-for-providers/">the ABDM onboarding process for a provider</a>.
</p>
