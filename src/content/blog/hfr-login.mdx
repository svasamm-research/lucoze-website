<h2 id="where">Where is the HFR login?</h2>
<p>
	The Health Facility Registry login is at{" "}
	<a href="https://facility.abdm.gov.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">
		facility.abdm.gov.in
	</a>
	. You sign in with the <strong>mobile number linked to your facility's ABDM account</strong> and a
	one-time password (OTP) sent to that number. There is no separate password to remember for most
	facilities — the OTP is the key, so the phone that holds that number is what matters.
</p>

<h2 id="steps">How to log in, step by step</h2>
<p>Portals get redesigned, so treat this as the shape of the flow, not fixed screens:</p>
<ol>
	<li>
		Open{" "}
		<a href="https://facility.abdm.gov.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">
			facility.abdm.gov.in
		</a>{" "}
		and choose to sign in.
	</li>
	<li>Enter the <strong>registered mobile number</strong> for your facility.</li>
	<li>Enter the <strong>OTP</strong> sent to that number.</li>
	<li>You land in your facility dashboard, where you manage your entry and your HFR ID.</li>
</ol>
<p>
	If your facility isn't registered yet, there is nothing to log in to — you create the entry first.
	We walk through that in{" "}
	<a href="/in/blog/register-clinic-on-hfr/">how to register your clinic on the HFR</a>.
</p>

<h2 id="cant-login">"I can't log in" — the usual reasons</h2>
<p>
	Almost every stuck login comes down to <strong>which number the account is on</strong>, not a
	forgotten password:
</p>
<ul>
	<li>
		<strong>OTP going to a number you no longer have.</strong> The account is tied to the mobile used
		at registration. If that person has left or the SIM is gone, the OTP goes with it — you update
		the registered mobile through the portal's support/update flow, not by making a new account.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>Two entries for one facility.</strong> If someone registered a second time on a different
		number, you can end up with a duplicate. Use the one that carries your real, verified HFR ID and
		get the duplicate deactivated rather than working both.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>OTP not arriving.</strong> Usually network or a full inbox; wait for the resend timer and
		check the number is entered correctly before assuming the account is broken.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>Wrong registry entirely.</strong> Trying to log a doctor in here won't work — see below.
	</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="three-logins">HFR, HPR and ABHA are three different logins</h2>
<p>The single most common mix-up. They are separate registries with separate sign-ins:</p>
<ul>
	<li>
		<strong>HFR</strong> — the <strong>facility</strong> login, here at facility.abdm.gov.in.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>HPR</strong> — the <strong>doctor's</strong> login, on the Healthcare Professionals
		Registry at hpr.abdm.gov.in. If you're trying to sign a clinician in, that's the one you want —
		see{" "}
		<a href="/in/blog/register-doctors-on-hpr/">HPR registration for doctors</a>.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>ABHA</strong> — the <strong>patient's</strong> health ID, which they create themselves.
	</li>
</ul>
<p>
	If you're new to all three and want the plain version of what each one is, start with{" "}
	<a href="/in/blog/hfr-explained/">HFR full form and HFR ID, explained</a>.
</p>

<h2 id="lucoze">Where this fits day to day</h2>
<p>
	The HFR login is a place you visit occasionally — to register, update details, or check your entry.
	The everyday work is issuing ABHA-linked records against that HFR ID, and that shouldn't mean
	logging into a government portal each time. Lucoze is built so issuing those records is part of the
	normal front-desk workflow, with data hosted in India. If you'd like to see how that works for your
	hospital, <a href="/in/demo/">book a demo</a>.
</p>
