<h2 id="what-it-is">What is HFR (and what isn't it)?</h2>
<p>
	The <strong>Health Facility Registry (HFR)</strong> is the public list of verified health
	facilities under the{" "}
	<a href="/in/blog/abdm-abha-guide/">Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM)</a>, run by the
	National Health Authority (NHA). Hospitals, clinics, diagnostic centres, pharmacies, labs — if it
	delivers care, it can have an HFR entry with a unique facility ID.
</p>
<p>
	Think of it as the facility-side twin of ABHA. ABHA identifies the patient; HFR identifies the
	place. Once your clinic is registered and verified, it becomes discoverable within ABDM and can
	issue and verify ABHA-linked records on the standard rails.
</p>
<p>Here is what it is not:</p>
<ul>
	<li>
		It's not a clinical licence. HFR does not replace your local registration, NABH, or state health
		authority approvals — it sits alongside them.
	</li>
	<li>
		It's not a data upload. Registering a facility doesn't hand your patient records to anyone.
		Records stay where they're created.
	</li>
	<li>
		It's not the same as HPR. HFR is the <em>facility</em>; the Healthcare Professional Registry
		(HPR) is the <em>doctor</em>. You typically want both.
	</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="why">Why should a clinic register on HFR?</h2>
<p>
	Being on HFR is the difference between saying you're "ABDM-ready" and actually being on the map. A
	verified facility ID is what lets your clinic issue records patients can pull into their ABHA,
	verify other providers, and participate in consent-based exchange. Without it, you're outside the
	network looking in.
</p>
<p>Concretely, it matters when:</p>
<ul>
	<li>
		A patient asks you to link a visit or a report to their ABHA — you can only do that from a
		registered facility.
	</li>
	<li>
		State insurance and scheme workflows increasingly key off ABDM identifiers. Being registered
		keeps you inside the digital lane instead of the paper one.
	</li>
	<li>
		Referrals and cross-clinic exchange under ABDM need a verified sender and receiver. HFR is your
		identity on that network.
	</li>
</ul>
<blockquote>
	If your software vendor says you're "ABDM-ready" but your facility isn't in HFR, that readiness is
	theoretical. HFR is the row that makes it real.
</blockquote>

<h2 id="what-you-need">What do you need before you start?</h2>
<p>
	The exact document list varies by facility type; broadly, have ready:
</p>
<ul>
	<li>
		A <strong>mobile number</strong> you control (OTP-based sign-in), and ideally the owner's or an
		authorised person's details.
	</li>
	<li>
		Your <strong>facility's legal and operational details</strong> — name, type (clinic, polyclinic,
		nursing home, diagnostic centre), ownership (private/public), and specialties offered.
	</li>
	<li>
		The <strong>full address</strong> with pincode, and location that matches your signboard reality
		— this is what gets verified.
	</li>
	<li>
		Any <strong>registration or licence numbers</strong> your facility already holds (local
		registration, GST if applicable, and similar) — the portal will tell you which are mandatory for
		your facility type.
	</li>
	<li>
		Basic <strong>infrastructure details</strong> — beds if any, key services, operating hours.
	</li>
</ul>
<p>
	If you're unsure which documents apply to your facility type, don't guess — the portal branches
	the form by facility category and tells you what's required. Check there.
</p>

<h2 id="flow">How does HFR registration work, step by step?</h2>
<p>
	The process lives entirely on the official portal. Rather than invent field names or step counts,
	here's the arc to expect:
</p>
<ul>
	<li>
		Go to{" "}
		<a href="https://facility.abdm.gov.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">
			facility.abdm.gov.in
		</a>{" "}
		and start registration for a new facility, signing in with your mobile via OTP.
	</li>
	<li>
		Enter facility details — type, ownership, specialties, address, and the identifiers the form
		asks for.
	</li>
	<li>
		Submit for <strong>verification</strong>. HFR entries are verified, not self-asserted — that's
		the whole point of the registry — so there's a review step before your facility is fully live.
	</li>
	<li>Once verified, your facility gets its HFR ID and appears in the registry.</li>
</ul>
<p>
	For the precise, current steps — button labels, mandatory fields, what triggers verification, and
	any fees — follow the portal itself. It's the only source that stays up to date, and it's the one
	that counts.
</p>

<h2 id="after">What happens after you're registered?</h2>
<p>
	Registration is the start, not the finish. A live HFR ID is only useful if your systems actually
	use it:
</p>
<ul>
	<li>
		<strong>Record it.</strong> Keep the HFR facility ID somewhere your team and your software can
		reach — it's your identity on the network.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>Wire it into your EMR.</strong> The ID needs to flow into whatever issues ABHA-linked
		records. Registration alone doesn't make records appear in a patient's ABHA — your software has
		to do the linking.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>Register your doctors on HPR.</strong> HFR covers the facility; HPR covers the
		professionals. For full ABDM participation you generally want both.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>Keep it accurate.</strong> If you move, add specialties, or change ownership, update the
		entry. A stale registry row misdirects patients and referrals.
	</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="snags">What are the common snags?</h2>
<ul>
	<li>
		<strong>Picking the wrong facility type.</strong> The form branches on this, and it drives which
		documents you need. Get it right the first time or you'll redo fields.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>Address that doesn't match reality.</strong> Verification checks that the facility
		exists where you say it does. A sloppy or mismatched address is the most common reason things
		stall.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>Confusing HFR with HPR.</strong> Registering the facility does not register your
		doctors, and vice versa. Two registries, two jobs.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>Assuming registration = integration.</strong> Being in HFR doesn't automatically issue
		ABHA-linked records. Your EMR has to support ABDM and use the facility ID. If your vendor can't
		tell you how, that's your answer.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>Trusting a screenshot from 2023.</strong> The portal evolves. Anything you read about
		exact steps — including this post — is a map, not the territory. Confirm on the portal.
	</li>
</ul>
