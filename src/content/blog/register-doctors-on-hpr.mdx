<h2 id="what">What is the HPR?</h2>
<p>
	<strong>HPR</strong> is the <strong>Healthcare Professionals Registry</strong> — the verified list
	of individual clinicians under the{" "}
	<a href="/in/blog/abdm-abha-guide/">Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM)</a>, run by the
	National Health Authority (NHA). If the{" "}
	<a href="/in/blog/hfr-explained/">Health Facility Registry (HFR)</a> is the ID for the{" "}
	<em>place</em>, the HPR is the ID for the <em>person</em> giving care — the doctor, and in time
	nurses, pharmacists and other professionals.
</p>
<p>
	Each registered professional gets a verified <strong>HPR ID</strong>, tied to their medical
	council registration. It stays with the clinician even if they move hospitals — it's theirs, not
	the facility's.
</p>

<h2 id="why">Why does a doctor need an HPR ID?</h2>
<p>
	Because a record needs a verified author. When a discharge summary or prescription is issued to a
	patient's ABHA, ABDM wants to know a real, registration-verified clinician stands behind it — not
	just a name typed into a box. Practically, for a hospital:
</p>
<ul>
	<li>It's what lets your doctors sign records that flow into patients' ABHA accounts.</li>
	<li>
		It's half of "being on ABDM" — the facility on HFR, the clinicians on HPR. One without the other
		is incomplete.
	</li>
	<li>
		It builds a verifiable identity for each doctor across the health system, independent of any one
		employer.
	</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="how">How does HPR registration work?</h2>
<p>
	Registration happens at{" "}
	<a href="https://hpr.abdm.gov.in/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">
		hpr.abdm.gov.in
	</a>
	. In shape, it is:
</p>
<ul>
	<li>
		The clinician signs up (usually with mobile/Aadhaar and an OTP) and creates their HPR profile.
	</li>
	<li>
		They enter their council registration details — the State/National Medical Council number and
		year.
	</li>
	<li>Those details are verified against the council record, and the HPR ID is issued.</li>
</ul>
<p>
	It's a per-doctor step, done once. The exact screens change with portal updates, so confirm the
	current flow on the registry itself.
</p>

<h2 id="hospital">Whose job is it — the doctor's or the hospital's?</h2>
<p>
	Formally the HPR ID belongs to the clinician, so each doctor registers themselves. But in a busy
	hospital that rarely happens on its own. The hospitals that get this done treat it as an
	onboarding step — the administrator keeps a simple list of who has an HPR ID and helps new joiners
	get one, the same way they'd collect a council registration copy. It sits inside the wider{" "}
	<a href="/in/blog/abdm-onboarding-for-providers/">ABDM onboarding sequence</a>: facility on HFR,
	doctors on HPR, ABHAs created at the front desk.
</p>
