<p>
	The most expensive line item in an Indian clinic is one that never appears on any invoice: the
	cost of staying on software you have outgrown, because switching feels too risky.
</p>
<p>
	We have spoken to clinic owners across Kolkata, Patna, Bhubaneswar, and Lucknow who openly dislike
	their current system — the billing leaks, the clunky front desk, the missing ABDM support, the
	per-user pricing that creeps up every quarter. And yet they stay. For years.
</p>
<p>The reason is almost never price or features. It is fear of the switch.</p>
<p>This article is the honest, hour-by-hour answer to that fear.</p>

<h2 id="nightmare">The nightmare every clinic owner imagines</h2>
<p>
	When we ask clinic owners what stops them from changing software, the answer comes in some version
	of the same story:
</p>
<blockquote>
	“We will lose patient data. The front desk will collapse for a week. Appointments will go
	missing. Patients will walk out the door. And it will be my fault.”
</blockquote>
<p>
	It is a completely rational fear. A clinic is a live system. Patients arrive every few minutes.
	The front desk cannot pause. Unlike a retail business that can shut for a stocktake, a clinic has
	to keep running while it changes the engine underneath it.
</p>
<p>
	So the question is not “is switching scary?” Of course it is. The real question is:{" "}
	<strong>can the switch be run in a way where the clinic never stops functioning?</strong>
</p>
<p>
	After running this migration with six East-India clinics in 2026, our answer is yes — if it is
	run properly. Here is what “properly” actually means.
</p>

<h2 id="14-days">What the 14 days actually look like</h2>
<p>
	The core principle is simple:{" "}
	<strong>your old system stays live as a safety net until you no longer need it.</strong> There is no
	“big bang” cutover weekend where everything moves at once and you pray it works. The transition is
	gradual, validated at each step, and reversible until the final day.
</p>

<h2 id="day-0">Day 0 — You export from your current system</h2>
<p>
	A 20-minute task on your side. Most clinic platforms used in India — Practo Ray, Halemind,
	Cliniq360 — provide CSV exports we can read directly. You stay in full control of your own data
	the entire time.
</p>

<h2 id="days-1-3">Days 1 to 3 — We import and validate</h2>
<p>
	We bring your patient master in first, because it is the foundation everything else hangs from.
	Then appointment history. Active and upcoming bookings roll forward, with confirmation messages
	going out to patients so nobody is left wondering whether their appointment survived the move.
</p>
<p>
	Throughout these three days, <strong>your old system remains your primary system.</strong> Nothing
	depends on the new platform yet. We are building a verified parallel copy while your clinic runs
	exactly as it did the week before.
</p>

<h2 id="days-4-7">Days 4 to 7 — Billing cutover</h2>
<p>
	This is the first live operational change. On the morning of Day 4, the front desk begins taking
	payments on the new system. Billing is the right first step because it is high-frequency — your
	team gets comfortable fast through sheer repetition.
</p>
<p>
	Critically, the old system stays open as a safety net for the entire week. If anything feels off,
	the fallback is one click away. In practice, by Day 6 most front desks have stopped opening the
	old system at all — not because they were told to, but because the new one is faster.
</p>

<h2 id="days-8-14">Days 8 to 14 — Full operations</h2>
<p>
	Lab and pharmacy modules go live by Day 10. Doctors are writing notes and prescriptions on the new
	system by Day 12. By Day 14, the old system is archived — not deleted, archived — and the clinic
	is running fully on the new platform.
</p>
<p>
	At no point in these 14 days is there a single day where the clinic cannot function. That is the
	whole design.
</p>

<h2 id="migration-fee">The migration fee question</h2>
<p>
	There is one more thing worth saying plainly, because it is where a lot of clinic software quietly
	extracts money: <strong>migration should not cost you anything.</strong>
</p>
<p>
	We do not charge a migration fee. It would be unreasonable to charge a clinic for the privilege of
	moving its own data from one place to another. Your patient records belong to you and your
	patients. Moving them to a better home is not a billable service — it is the bare minimum a
	software partner owes you.
</p>
<p>
	If a vendor quotes you a migration or “onboarding” fee that runs into lakhs, that fee is not
	covering real cost. It is covering the fact that they know switching is painful, and they are
	pricing your fear.
</p>

<h2 id="questions">What to ask before you switch (whether or not you choose us)</h2>
<p>
	If you are evaluating any clinic software in 2026 — not just ours — these are the questions that
	separate a safe migration from a risky one:
</p>
<ol>
	<li>
		<strong>“Does my old system stay live during the transition?”</strong> If the answer involves a
		single cutover day with no fallback, walk carefully.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>“Can you read an export from my current platform?”</strong> If they cannot import Practo
		Ray or Halemind CSVs, your data migration becomes manual and error-prone.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>“Is migration included, or is there a fee?”</strong> A migration fee is a fear tax. Know
		what you are paying for.
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>“Where will my patient data be hosted?”</strong> Under the Digital Personal Data
		Protection Act 2023, patient health records are sensitive personal data. Data hosted inside
		India keeps your compliance posture clean. (We host every byte in Mumbai — AWS ap-south-1 — and
		nothing crosses the border.)
	</li>
	<li>
		<strong>“If I ever want to leave you, how do I get my data out?”</strong> The answer should be a
		clean CSV and PDF export within 24 hours, with a grace period after cancellation. No lock-in, no
		proprietary format.
	</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="closing">The fear is real. The nightmare is not.</h2>
<p>
	Switching clinic software is genuinely daunting. We are not going to pretend otherwise — that is
	not how we talk to clinic owners.
</p>
<p>
	But the specific nightmare — lost data, a collapsed front desk, patients walking out — is an
	artifact of bad migrations, not of migration itself. Run the switch gradually, keep the old system
	as a safety net, validate at every step, and charge nothing for moving the data, and the 14 days
	become a controlled, boring, uneventful process. Boring is exactly what you want when patient care
	is on the line.
</p>
<p>
	If fear of the switch is the only thing keeping your clinic on software you have outgrown, that
	fear is costing you more than any subscription ever will.
</p>
